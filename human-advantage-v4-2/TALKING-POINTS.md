# The Human Advantage v4.2 — talking points

## Through-line

Better operations start before the KPI moves. The deck recognises the human attention, trust and small actions that keep patient promises moving.

## Slide 1 — Look how far we came

Open with evidence rather than nostalgia. Many former concerns and workshop questions are now normal parts of the operation. This is progress created by people.

## Slide 2 — Three chapters

Use the approved timeline labels: **2024**, **2025**, **Now**. In 2024 the focus was designing the operating model. By 2025 capability was landing. Now the promise and responsibility are much bigger.

## Slide 3 — Yesterday’s ambition became today’s baseline

Ideas became operating capability, then the finish line moved again. Pause to recognise the people behind that progress.

## Slide 4 — The Human Advantage

Turn from the operating system to the human system underneath it: attention, trust and the energy people bring to the work.

## Slide 5 — 18,000 quiet promises

An order is a patient waiting, a safe dispensing decision, stock reconciled and several handoffs kept intact. Good operations often makes effort invisible.

## Slide 6 — This year asked a lot

Name the restructure and redundancies plainly. Gratitude that ignores the cost is avoidance. We can be proud of the response without pretending the year did not hurt.

## Slide 7 — Recognise progress

Use the simplified message: we do not need to wait for the finish line to recognise progress. Recognition is not a reward for being finished; it gives people energy for the next improvement.

## Slide 8 — Take the next useful action

Use the simplified practical-optimism wording: see the problem clearly and take the next useful action. Make the five responsibilities concrete: allocate demand, maintain supply, resolve exceptions, support partners and protect patients.

## Slide 9 — We are starting from proof

Keep this brisk. Each example represents people noticing friction, coordinating across teams and staying with the work.

## Slide 10 — The work nobody sees

Big wins make the deck. Teams are held together by small actions that prevent visible failure. Recognition should make invisible operational care visible.

## Slide 11 — Lollipop moments

Invite people to think of a specific moment when someone here made the work, the year or a difficult day better for them. Offer the examples on screen: stepping into a gap, explaining something patiently, noticing a risk, supporting someone through change, or protecting a patient or colleague through a small action. Give people quiet time to recall one.

## Slide 12 — Make the impact visible

Facilitate a short reflection. Ask people to write down the person, what they did and why it mattered, then tell them. Sharing is invited, not forced; private thanks count.

## Slide 13 — Thank you

Thank every group, not just leaders. Add two genuine examples if available. End by thanking people for showing up for patients and pharmacies while the organisation changed around them.

## Slide 14 — We do not wait

Close by holding the final idea: we recognise each other before the work becomes easy, and that recognition is part of how we make the operation better.
